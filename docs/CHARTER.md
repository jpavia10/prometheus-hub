# PROMETHEUS — MASTER EVOLUTION CHARTER
## A mandate for the transformation of Prometheus into an exceptional personal digital ecosystem

**Issued:** November 12, 2026
**Audience:** Fable, Muse, Claude, other contributing agents, and future developers
**Authority:** This document establishes the product vision, design principles, architectural direction, quality standards, and long-term ambitions for Prometheus.

---

# 1. THE AMBITION

Do not think of Prometheus as a dashboard.

Do not think of it as a collection of utility pages.

Do not think of it as a conventional productivity application with a futuristic appearance.

**Think of Prometheus as a personal digital ecosystem: an evolving environment in which specialized systems work together to help one person understand, manage, create, preserve, and improve the things that matter in their life.**

The existing Hub and its pillars are the beginning of that vision, not its limits.

The ambition is to build an environment that feels as though it belongs several generations ahead of ordinary personal software: sophisticated, beautiful, intelligent, cohesive, deeply useful, and unusually enjoyable to inhabit.

It should combine the best qualities of:

- The clarity, responsiveness, and craftsmanship of exceptional native applications.
- The information density and analytical power of professional instruments.
- The flexibility and extensibility of modern development platforms.
- The spatial imagination of 3D and immersive computing.
- The interconnected knowledge structures of advanced research tools.
- The speed and discoverability of excellent command-driven interfaces.
- The reliability and user ownership of local-first software.
- The adaptability and potential of AI-assisted systems.

These are sources of inspiration, not templates to copy.

The goal is not to imitate Linear, Raycast, Obsidian, Apple, Bloomberg, or any other product. Study what makes exceptional products work, understand why their decisions are effective, and combine those principles into something distinctly Prometheus.

**Do not optimize for what an ordinary application would look like. Discover what the best possible version of Prometheus could become.**

# 2. FIRST PRINCIPLE: THINK BEYOND THE REQUEST

The user does not necessarily know which capabilities, technologies, interfaces, or architectural improvements are possible.

That is part of your responsibility.

Do not interpret a request narrowly when a broader solution would create substantially more value.

When asked to improve a pillar, consider the entire experience. When asked to improve the Hub, consider the ecosystem. When asked to add a feature, consider whether a better underlying capability would solve several problems at once.

Actively identify:

- Missing capabilities the user has not thought to request.
- Unnecessary friction in existing workflows.
- Opportunities to combine information across pillars.
- Repetitive work that could be automated.
- Data that could become more useful when connected.
- Better ways to represent complex information.
- New interaction methods that would make the environment more intuitive.
- Architectural improvements that unlock future capabilities.
- Entirely new pillars that would fill genuine gaps.
- Opportunities to make the experience more memorable and delightful.

Do not merely execute instructions. Exercise informed judgment.

When you identify a potentially transformative opportunity, explain it, evaluate its feasibility, and recommend it—even if it was not requested.

Do not agree with weak ideas simply because they were suggested. Challenge them constructively.

Do not mistake novelty for progress. Every addition must justify its complexity.

# 3. THE PROMETHEUS EXPERIENCE

Prometheus should feel like a coherent civilization of specialized environments.

The Hub is the central command environment. The pillars are specialized destinations. Shared infrastructure connects them. Each pillar has its own purpose, identity, atmosphere, and way of working.

The experience should have three complementary qualities:

**A. A unified world**

There should be an unmistakable Prometheus identity. Navigation, transitions, system behavior, shared data, typography principles, and interaction conventions should feel related.

**B. Distinct worlds within it**

Each pillar should feel purpose-built. A financial environment should not feel like a book archive with different colors. A vehicle environment should not feel like a generic dashboard. An intellectual environment should not feel like a form builder.

**C. An invisible layer of intelligence**

The system should make it easier to understand relationships, find information, recognize important changes, and move between related activities.

The user should not need to understand the architecture to benefit from it.

The environment should feel sophisticated without making ordinary tasks difficult.

# 4. THE CENTRAL HUB: A COMMAND ENVIRONMENT, NOT A LAUNCHER

The Hub must become substantially more than a screen containing links to pillars.

It should provide a meaningful overview of the entire ecosystem.

Explore the following capabilities and determine which belong in the immediate experience and which should be developed progressively.

### 4.1 A living system overview

The Hub should understand the state of the ecosystem.

Potential elements include:

- Pillar status and recent activity.
- Important upcoming events and deadlines.
- Cross-pillar activity.
- Current projects and ongoing priorities.
- Relevant changes since the previous visit.
- Data freshness and system health.
- Pending tasks, unresolved issues, and important alerts.
- Recently accessed environments.
- A personalized overview that changes according to context.

Do not turn this into an overcrowded wall of widgets. Develop a hierarchy that makes the most important information immediately apparent.

### 4.2 Advanced navigation

Investigate a command palette that can become the fastest way to navigate and act throughout Prometheus.

Consider:

- Universal search.
- Instant pillar navigation.
- Search across permitted pillar data.
- Global quick capture.
- Context-sensitive commands.
- Recently used actions.
- Keyboard shortcuts.
- Natural-language input where genuinely useful.
- Suggestions based on the user's current context.

A command interface should complement visual navigation, not replace it.

### 4.3 Spatial identity

Preserve and elevate the existing orrery, particle-field environment, and sense of spatial depth.

Investigate whether the Hub can become more expressive through:

- Sophisticated lighting and environmental effects.
- Meaningful motion and transitions.
- Distinctive representations of pillars.
- Interactive relationships between systems.
- Subtle changes reflecting system activity.
- Optional alternate navigation arrangements.
- More advanced spatial representations as hardware and browser capabilities evolve.

Every visual effect must have a purpose. The experience must remain fast, legible, and comfortable.

### 4.4 A genuine home environment

The Hub should make returning to Prometheus feel rewarding.

Consider a carefully composed arrival experience, a useful daily overview, a clear sense of where the user left off, and fast access to ongoing work.

Avoid turning the arrival screen into a mandatory ceremony. Users should be able to reach their destination immediately.

# 5. THE PILLAR SYSTEM

Every pillar is an individual product within the larger ecosystem.

The shared framework should provide capabilities, not dictate the complete appearance or behavior of every environment.

Each pillar must have a documented identity and purpose.

For each one, establish:

1. Its mission.
2. Its primary users and use cases.
3. Its essential workflows.
4. Its information architecture.
5. Its distinctive visual language.
6. Its signature interaction.
7. Its most valuable visualizations.
8. Its relationship to other pillars.
9. Its data ownership and storage requirements.
10. Its future expansion opportunities.

A pillar should not exist merely because a category sounds useful.

It should solve a real problem exceptionally well.

### 5.1 Existing pillar responsibilities

Preserve the established roles and boundaries.

- **Kairos:** Time, calendar, scheduling, and temporal awareness.
- **Midas:** Financial environment. Improve the surrounding experience without modifying protected internals.
- **Titan:** Titan Reliquary. Locked against unauthorized modification. Do not alter its established contracts or introduce regressions.
- **Hearth:** Home and household environment.
- **Garage:** Vehicles and automotive information.
- **Apollo:** Health, fitness, and personal performance capabilities within its established scope.
- **Payoff:** Debt, payoff planning, and financial projections.
- **Selling:** Selling and transaction workflows.
- **Records:** Important records and documentation.
- **Icarus:** Preserve its established purpose; investigate opportunities only after verifying its current responsibilities.
- **Hermes:** Communications and correspondence capabilities within its established scope.
- **Piano:** Music and practice environment.
- **Bastion:** Preparedness, resilience, and readiness.
- **Athenaeum:** Intellectual archive and knowledge environment.
- **Odyssey:** Travel and exploration.
- **Privacy:** Privacy and personal data protection.
- **Argus:** Monitoring, observation, and awareness within its established scope.
- **Sylade:** Presence-only. Do not expand its responsibilities.

The current source of truth for pillar names, ownership, status, and contracts must be verified against the repository. Do not assume this list resolves every implementation detail.

Other established boundaries:

- Fleet remains paused until explicitly reactivated.
- The Trading Journal remains an analytical journal and research environment. It must not execute trades or provide trading advice.
- Midas protected internals remain protected.
- Titan remains locked.
- Sylade remains presence-only.

Do not use this transformation mandate as permission to override those restrictions.

### 5.2 Make each pillar exceptional

Examples of the level of ambition expected:

**Athenaeum — An evolving intellectual universe**

Go beyond notes and folders.

Explore a visual knowledge graph, interconnected ideas, research trails, source provenance, evolving arguments, timelines, concept relationships, and the ability to follow an idea from initial capture through refinement and application.

Preserve original thinking. Distinguish original ideas from external sources, interpretations, and AI-generated contributions.

**Midas — A sophisticated financial instrument**

Think beyond tables of balances.

Explore financial timelines, scenario modeling, cash-flow visualization, asset and liability relationships, projections, and clear explanations of how changes affect the larger picture.

Respect protected internals and data boundaries.

**Garage — A vehicle intelligence environment**

Think beyond a list of vehicles and maintenance dates.

Explore vehicle profiles, service histories, mileage timelines, maintenance forecasting, documentation, cost histories, and visual representations that make a vehicle's condition and ownership history easy to understand.

**Piano — An interactive music studio**

Think beyond a practice checklist.

Explore practice sessions, progress visualization, repertoire, structured learning, musical reference material, and useful interactive tools.

**Bastion — A personal resilience environment**

Think beyond a collection of emergency supplies.

Explore preparedness inventories, readiness assessments, maintenance schedules, household dependencies, plans, and clear visualizations of preparedness gaps.

These are examples of product ambition, not instructions to implement every listed feature immediately.

### 5.3 Create new pillars when justified

The current collection must not become a constraint on the future.

Conduct an ecosystem gap analysis. Determine whether important areas of personal life, knowledge, work, creativity, ownership, or system management lack a suitable environment.

Investigate possibilities such as:

**Forge — AI and agent orchestration**

A dedicated environment for managing AI-assisted work, agent assignments, project queues, reviews, outputs, and cross-agent collaboration.

It could become a control center for work distributed among multiple AI systems, with clear task ownership, provenance, review status, and approval boundaries.

Do not make it a superficial chat aggregator.

**Continuity — Personal archive and life history**

A carefully designed environment for preserving important events, decisions, milestones, personal records, and meaningful context over time.

Its purpose would be long-term continuity and retrieval, not duplicating Athenaeum.

**Vitality — A deeper personal performance environment**

Only if Apollo's current scope cannot adequately serve the need. Explore whether a dedicated environment for long-term health, activity, recovery, and performance would create enough additional value to justify separation.

**Foundry — Creative and experimental workspace**

A possible environment for developing ideas, prototypes, visual experiments, and creative projects that do not belong in an existing pillar.

**Observatory — External awareness and research**

A possible environment for monitoring selected developments, sources, subjects, and trends, with clear source attribution and distinction between verified information and interpretation.

These are candidate directions, not predetermined additions.

Evaluate them against existing pillars before proposing new ones. Merge overlapping responsibilities where appropriate.

A new pillar must demonstrate a distinct mission, meaningful workflows, data ownership, integration value, and long-term usefulness.

Do not create pillars simply to increase the count.

# 6. THE MISSING LAYER: CROSS-PILLAR INTELLIGENCE

This is one of the most important opportunities for Prometheus.

The pillars should not be isolated applications that happen to share a navigation screen.

They should be independently useful environments that can participate in a larger system.

Develop an explicit integration architecture.

### 6.1 Shared event system

Investigate a common event model through which pillars can publish meaningful events.

Examples:

- A new deadline appears in Kairos.
- A maintenance event is recorded in Garage.
- A document is added to Records.
- A financial milestone changes in Payoff.
- A new research item is captured in Athenaeum.

The Hub can use these events to provide relevant awareness without taking ownership of the underlying data.

Define event schemas, identifiers, timestamps, origin, versioning, and permissions.

### 6.2 Cross-pillar relationships

Investigate useful relationships between environments.

Examples:

- Household projects and their associated records.
- Vehicle expenses and financial history.
- Travel plans and calendar events.
- Research and the decisions it informs.
- AI-generated work and the projects that requested it.

The system should help the user discover relationships that would otherwise remain hidden.

Do not force artificial connections between unrelated information.

### 6.3 Universal search

Develop a search architecture that can discover information across the ecosystem while respecting pillar ownership.

It should be possible to find an item without remembering which pillar contains it.

Search results should identify their source and provide a direct route to the authoritative record.

### 6.4 Shared activity and timeline

Explore a unified activity history that shows meaningful changes across the ecosystem.

The user should be able to understand what happened, when it happened, which pillar was involved, and where to investigate further.

### 6.5 Contextual actions

A shared action framework could allow the Hub to invoke appropriate pillar actions without duplicating their logic.

For example, a global capture action might route a new item to the appropriate destination.

The receiving pillar remains responsible for its own data and business rules.

# 7. ARCHITECTURE FOR THE FUTURE

The existing architecture should be treated as a foundation to improve, not something to discard for the sake of a rewrite.

The repository currently establishes a static GitHub Pages environment, shared browser-side utilities, local storage, export/import capabilities, and common presentation infrastructure.

The available source inspection and automated proofs provide a useful starting point, but they do not establish that every pillar, workflow, or visual experience has been independently validated.

Perform a complete source and runtime audit before making architectural decisions.

### 7.1 Architectural principles

Preserve these core characteristics:

- Static GitHub Pages deployment as the current baseline.
- No mandatory backend or account requirement.
- Local-first operation.
- User ownership of data.
- Versioned export and import.
- Explicit pillar ownership.
- Isolated pillar business logic.
- Shared infrastructure with stable contracts.
- No fabricated integrations.
- Migration and regression protection.

### 7.2 Evolve the technical foundation

Investigate the following capabilities and introduce them where justified:

**A. Pillar manifests and registration**

Create a formal way to describe each pillar.

A manifest could declare its identity, routes, capabilities, data schemas, permissions, event subscriptions, integrations, dependencies, status, and version.

The Hub should discover registered pillars rather than rely on scattered hardcoded assumptions.

**B. Shared service layer**

Develop reusable services for:

- Data persistence.
- Search and indexing.
- Events and notifications.
- Import/export.
- Schema validation.
- Migrations.
- Preferences.
- Theme and visual settings.
- Command registration.
- Error reporting.
- Diagnostics.

Shared services must remain lightweight and independently testable.

**C. Data integrity and history**

Investigate:

- Versioned schemas.
- Explicit migrations.
- Backup verification.
- Import previews.
- Duplicate detection.
- Recovery from failed imports.
- Data integrity checks.
- Change history for important records.
- Provenance for externally sourced information.
- Clear separation of user-created, imported, calculated, and AI-generated data.

For critical records, preserving the ability to understand how a value originated is more important than merely displaying it.

**D. Storage evolution**

LocalStorage is useful for simple applications but should not automatically become the permanent storage solution for every pillar.

Evaluate IndexedDB or other browser-native storage where data volume, search, structured records, images, and performance justify it.

Do not migrate merely because a technology is newer.

**E. Optional synchronization**

Design the architecture so that future synchronization between devices is possible without making it a prerequisite for the current application.

Investigate conflict handling, stable identifiers, offline changes, data ownership, and synchronization states.

Do not promise seamless synchronization without implementing and validating it.

**F. Progressive web application capabilities**

Evaluate service workers, offline caching, installability, application updates, and graceful recovery.

The application should remain useful when disconnected.

**G. External integrations**

Use explicit adapters for external services.

Every integration must clearly identify:

- What information it accesses.
- Whether it is read-only or can perform actions.
- What permissions it requires.
- Whether data leaves the device.
- How failures are handled.
- How the user can disconnect it.

Do not expose API keys or private credentials in public client-side code.

**H. AI integration architecture**

Treat AI as an optional capability that can enhance multiple pillars.

Investigate a provider-independent adapter model with explicit permissions and structured inputs and outputs.

AI-generated content must be distinguishable from verified records and original user content.

Important actions should require appropriate confirmation.

Never allow an AI agent to silently rewrite authoritative personal records.

### 7.3 Respect technical reality

Prometheus should be ambitious, not delusional.

A static website cannot securely perform every operation that a backend service can. A browser cannot guarantee background execution when it is closed. A local application cannot synchronize devices without some communication mechanism.

Identify these constraints early.

Where a capability requires a future backend, external service, or additional permission, design a clean extension point and explain the tradeoff.

Do not compromise the current local-first experience to pursue speculative features.

# 8. THE DESIGN SYSTEM: COHESION WITHOUT HOMOGENIZATION

The existing shared CSS and component framework provide efficiency and consistency.

However, a shared foundation must not force every pillar into the same visual composition.

Separate three things:

**1. Prometheus identity**

Shared principles, interaction conventions, typography standards, navigation, motion behavior, and environmental continuity.

**2. Pillar identity**

Distinct visual compositions, signature visualizations, specialized controls, appropriate materials, and unique atmosphere.

**3. Functional consistency**

Common actions should behave consistently. Similar controls should look and behave predictably. A unique appearance must never make ordinary interactions confusing.

### 8.1 A visual design brief for every pillar

Before major implementation, define:

- Core visual metaphor.
- Desired emotional atmosphere.
- Primary composition.
- Typography and hierarchy.
- Color system.
- Signature visualization.
- Navigation model.
- Interaction patterns.
- Animation and transition language.
- Mobile composition.
- Desktop composition.
- Accessibility considerations.

Do not settle for changing accent colors.

### 8.2 Visual quality

Explore sophisticated design techniques where they improve the experience:

- Custom data visualizations.
- Spatial diagrams.
- Layered environments.
- Purposeful lighting.
- Subtle depth and parallax.
- Fluid transitions.
- High-quality typography.
- Carefully composed negative space.
- Meaningful ambient animation.
- Context-sensitive information density.
- Adaptive layouts.
- Interactive visual models.

Do not make every screen a 3D experiment.

Use spatial presentation when spatial relationships help people understand information. Use conventional layouts when they make work faster.

### 8.3 Mobile and desktop are first-class

The Galaxy S24 Ultra is an important real-world target, but do not optimize exclusively for one device.

Design for:

- Mobile phones.
- Tablets.
- Desktop browsers.
- Large displays.
- Future device categories where practical.

Do not simply shrink a desktop layout to fit a phone.

Each screen size deserves deliberate composition and interaction.

# 9. INTERACTION AND INTELLIGENCE

The system should become faster and more natural to operate over time.

Investigate:

- Universal command palette.
- Contextual action menus.
- Natural-language quick capture.
- Keyboard navigation.
- Gesture-based interactions where appropriate.
- Saved views.
- Configurable workspaces.
- Smart filtering.
- Useful recommendations.
- Cross-pillar shortcuts.
- Undo and recovery.
- Clear loading, empty, error, and success states.

Consider personalization, but avoid creating a settings maze.

The system should remember useful preferences and restore the user's working context where appropriate.

AI assistance should be optional, transparent, and controllable.

Do not add a chatbot to every pillar simply because AI is available.

The best AI integration may be an invisible improvement to a workflow rather than a conversational interface.

# 10. PERFORMANCE, RELIABILITY, PRIVACY, AND ACCESSIBILITY

An extraordinary interface that is slow, fragile, or unreliable is not extraordinary.

Establish measurable engineering standards.

### Performance

- Aim for smooth interactions and approximately 60fps where practical.
- Avoid unnecessary rendering and animation work.
- Lazy-load expensive visual components.
- Keep initial loading lightweight.
- Optimize large datasets and image handling.
- Test on real mobile hardware.
- Monitor memory consumption and long-running sessions.

### Reliability

- No silent data loss.
- No destructive import without appropriate validation and recovery.
- Clear handling of malformed data.
- Graceful failure of optional integrations.
- Reliable navigation and restoration.
- Explicit error reporting.
- Tested backup and recovery procedures.

### Privacy and security

- Minimize collection and transmission of personal information.
- Keep local data local unless the user explicitly authorizes otherwise.
- Do not store secrets in public source code.
- Clearly distinguish local processing from external processing.
- Use safe rendering practices for imported and user-provided content.
- Establish permissions before allowing cross-pillar access.

### Accessibility

- Strong contrast.
- Readable typography.
- Keyboard accessibility.
- Clear focus indicators.
- Screen-reader-conscious structure.
- Reduced-motion support.
- Touch targets appropriate for mobile.
- No information conveyed by color alone.

These are fundamental requirements, not optional polish.

# 11. A NEW DEVELOPMENT PROCESS: INVENT, DESIGN, BUILD, PROVE

The current approach must evolve beyond repeatedly requesting visual improvements and accepting whatever appears.

Every major improvement should pass through a disciplined creative and engineering process.

### Phase 1 — Discovery

Inspect the actual repository, current application, relevant source files, live behavior, existing data contracts, and project constraints.

Do not rely on assumptions or old screenshots.

Identify what exists, what works, what is incomplete, and what is merely described.

### Phase 2 — Opportunity discovery

Ask what the experience could become.

Identify missing features, design opportunities, architectural limitations, and potential new capabilities.

Look beyond the immediate assignment.

### Phase 3 — Concept exploration

For major work, develop at least three genuinely different approaches.

They should differ in meaningful ways, not merely colors or minor layouts.

Compare their:

- Product usefulness.
- Originality.
- Visual potential.
- Ease of use.
- Technical feasibility.
- Long-term flexibility.
- Implementation cost.

Select the strongest direction and explain the decision.

### Phase 4 — Experience design

Define the actual screens, workflows, navigation, interactions, data requirements, and visual system.

Do not begin by generating generic cards and forms.

Use sketches, prototypes, screenshots, or working experiments when they help validate the concept.

### Phase 5 — Implementation

Build the strongest viable version.

Preserve existing contracts and isolate changes.

Do not introduce unnecessary dependencies or large rewrites without demonstrating their value.

### Phase 6 — Independent review

The implementing agent must not be the sole judge of its own work.

Use distinct reviewers for:

- Product quality and design.
- Functional correctness.
- Architecture and regression risk.
- Mobile and accessibility.
- Data integrity and security.

Use blind reviews where practical. Reviewers should assess the result independently rather than simply approve the implementer's explanation.

### Phase 7 — Evidence

Every major delivery must include:

- What changed.
- Why it changed.
- Relevant files and components.
- Screenshots at mobile and desktop sizes.
- Functional test results.
- Regression results.
- Known limitations.
- Unverified claims.
- Remaining work.

A passing automated test suite does not prove visual excellence.

A beautiful screenshot does not prove functionality.

Both must be evaluated.

# 12. QUALITY GATES

Introduce explicit acceptance standards.

A major experience should be evaluated across these dimensions:

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Visual distinction and craftsmanship | 20 |
| Functional completeness and usefulness | 20 |
| Interaction and workflow quality | 15 |
| Originality and product imagination | 15 |
| Architecture and maintainability | 10 |
| Reliability and data integrity | 10 |
| Performance, mobile behavior, and accessibility | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

Targets:

- 90+: Excellent; suitable for acceptance.
- 80–89: Strong, but meaningful deficiencies remain.
- 70–79: Functional progress, not yet exceptional.
- Below 70: Reconsider the approach or substantially improve it.

Do not inflate scores.

Critical failures override numerical scores. Data loss, security violations, broken pillar boundaries, serious regressions, and fabricated functionality block acceptance.

# 13. EXPANSION WITHOUT CHAOS

A growing ecosystem can become difficult to maintain if every new idea creates another isolated page.

Establish a formal capability lifecycle.

Every proposed feature or pillar should be classified as:

- Existing and operational.
- Existing but incomplete.
- Proposed.
- In development.
- Experimental.
- Paused.
- Deprecated.

Every capability should have an owner, a defined purpose, dependencies, and a clear status.

New pillars should go through a lightweight admission process:

1. Identify a genuine unmet need.
2. Confirm it does not duplicate an existing pillar.
3. Define its mission and boundaries.
4. Establish its data ownership.
5. Identify integration opportunities.
6. Prototype its experience.
7. Estimate its maintenance cost.
8. Approve it only if its long-term value justifies its existence.

Build a system that can grow without becoming a collection of abandoned experiments.

# 14. FUTURE TECHNOLOGY WATCH

Prometheus should be designed to evolve with technology.

Maintain an ongoing assessment of developments that could materially improve the ecosystem.

Investigate selectively:

- More advanced browser rendering and graphics.
- WebGPU and richer visual computing.
- Spatial and immersive interfaces.
- Improved local databases and search.
- Local AI models and on-device intelligence.
- AI agents and structured tool execution.
- Secure device-to-device communication.
- Optional synchronization systems.
- Better data visualization.
- Voice interfaces.
- Context-aware interactions.
- New accessibility capabilities.

Do not adopt technology because it is fashionable.

For each opportunity, determine whether it solves a real problem, whether the current platform can support it, and whether the benefit outweighs the complexity.

Design for future compatibility without prematurely building speculative infrastructure.

# 15. THE TRANSFORMATION ROADMAP

Do not attempt to rebuild everything simultaneously.

The following sequence is a strategic starting point. Validate it against the actual repository and adjust based on evidence.

**Stage 1 — Establish the truth**

Complete a comprehensive inventory of the repository, live application, all pillars, shared infrastructure, data ownership, and current tests.

Identify the strongest and weakest parts of the ecosystem.

Create a capability map and technical dependency map.

**Stage 2 — Establish the future architecture**

Define pillar manifests, shared services, event contracts, data ownership, universal navigation, search, and quality gates.

Identify which improvements can be made incrementally without disrupting working systems.

**Stage 3 — Prove the new standard**

Select a suitable active pillar as the design and architecture proving ground.

Athenaeum is a strong candidate because of its intellectual depth, interconnected information, and potential for distinctive visual interaction.

However, inspect the actual state and choose the best candidate based on impact and feasibility.

Build one genuinely exceptional experience, then extract reusable principles and components without making every other pillar look identical.

**Stage 4 — Elevate the Hub**

Use the proven architecture to make the Hub a true command environment, with meaningful system awareness, excellent navigation, contextual actions, and a distinctive arrival experience.

**Stage 5 — Transform active pillars**

Work through the remaining active pillars in a deliberate sequence.

Prioritize by user value, weakness of the current experience, technical readiness, and cross-pillar impact.

**Stage 6 — Introduce cross-pillar intelligence**

Develop shared events, activity history, universal search, contextual navigation, and useful relationships between environments.

**Stage 7 — Expand intelligently**

Consider new pillars only after the gap analysis.

Introduce future capabilities progressively and preserve the stability of the existing system.

**Stage 8 — Continuous evolution**

Establish a recurring cycle of discovery, design, implementation, independent evaluation, and refinement.

Prometheus should never be considered permanently finished.

# 16. NON-NEGOTIABLE PROTECTIONS

- Do not modify Titan Reliquary without explicit authorization.
- Do not modify protected Midas internals.
- Do not reactivate Fleet without explicit authorization.
- Do not expand Sylade beyond presence-only.
- Do not turn the Trading Journal into an execution or trading-advice system.
- Do not fabricate functionality, integrations, test results, or data.
- Do not silently change authoritative personal records.
- Do not sacrifice data integrity for visual improvements.
- Do not introduce a mandatory backend or account requirement without explicit approval.
- Do not replace the existing Prometheus identity with an unrelated design.
- Do not perform disruptive rewrites merely to create the appearance of progress.

When an improvement conflicts with an established protection, stop and explain the conflict.

# 17. WHAT SUCCESS SHOULD FEEL LIKE

The finished experience should produce several reactions.

**When entering the Hub:** It should feel like entering a sophisticated personal command environment, not opening a web dashboard.

**When entering a pillar:** It should feel like entering a specialized product designed specifically for that purpose.

**When using the system:** It should feel fast, intuitive, responsive, and unusually capable.

**When connecting information:** The user should discover useful relationships without having to manually coordinate every system.

**When preserving information:** The user should trust that important records remain accessible, understandable, portable, and under their control.

**When the ecosystem grows:** New capabilities should feel like natural extensions of an established system, not unrelated applications bolted together.

**When looking at the result:** It should be difficult to mistake Prometheus for a generic AI-generated project.

The quality should be apparent in the details, the architecture, the interactions, the visual identity, and the thought behind every decision.

# 18. IMMEDIATE ASSIGNMENT

Do not begin by changing colors, adding cards, or rewriting the entire application.

First, conduct a genuine transformation assessment.

Deliver these artifacts:

1. **Current-State Audit:** What actually exists, what works, what is incomplete, and what remains unverified.
2. **Ecosystem Map:** Every pillar, its purpose, status, dependencies, ownership, and relationships.
3. **Gap Analysis:** Important capabilities missing from the current system.
4. **Design Assessment:** Which environments feel generic, repetitive, incomplete, or visually underdeveloped.
5. **Architecture Assessment:** What currently enables the ecosystem and what restricts its future.
6. **New Pillar Proposals:** Only genuinely valuable additions, with clear justification and overlap analysis.
7. **Future Experience Concept:** A concrete vision for what the Hub and ecosystem could become.
8. **Transformation Roadmap:** A prioritized sequence with dependencies, risk, expected impact, and implementation effort.
9. **First Demonstration:** Identify one high-impact experience that can prove the new standard.

For every recommendation, distinguish between:

- Verified current capability.
- Reasonable inference.
- Proposed improvement.
- Longer-term possibility.

Do not claim to have inspected files, rendered screens, or tested functionality that you have not actually inspected, rendered, or tested.

Do not treat the existing automated proof suite as a substitute for independent visual and usability review.

After presenting the assessment, recommend the strongest next move. Do not wait for the user to identify every opportunity.

# FINAL MANDATE

**Build the Prometheus that the user would want if they could see all the possibilities available to them.**

Think bigger than the current implementation. Think beyond the current list of pillars. Think beyond conventional application design.

Bring product imagination, technical judgment, visual ambition, and disciplined engineering together.

Do not settle for adequate when a better approach is achievable.

Do not create complexity for its own sake.

Do not protect mediocre decisions from criticism.

Preserve what works. Improve what is weak. Invent what is missing. Make the whole greater than the sum of its parts.

The objective is not simply to make Prometheus look more futuristic.

**The objective is to build a genuinely extraordinary personal digital environment—one that becomes more useful, more capable, more coherent, and more impressive as it evolves.**
